The Basics

What Are Peptides?

Peptides are short chains of amino acids — the same building blocks that make up proteins. Unlike full proteins, peptides are small enough to cross biological barriers, making them exceptionally effective at signaling specific processes in the body.

Research peptides are synthetic versions of naturally occurring compounds, studied for their potential role in recovery, neuroprotection, tissue repair, and hormonal regulation. They are currently used in laboratory and research settings and are not FDA-approved for human therapeutic use outside of specific clinical contexts.

🔬

Targeted Signaling

Peptides communicate with specific receptors, triggering precise biological responses rather than broad systemic changes.

⚡

Rapid Bioavailability

Due to their small molecular size, peptides are absorbed and metabolized faster than traditional protein supplements.

🧬

Active Research

Dozens of peptides are under active clinical investigation for wound healing, cognitive enhancement, and anti-aging mechanisms.

Hormonal ✅ FDA Approved (Vyleesi)

PT-141

Bremelanotide — a melanocortin receptor agonist and the active compound in Vyleesi, FDA-approved for hypoactive sexual desire disorder. Acts centrally via the nervous system rather than vascular pathways — unlike PDE5 inhibitors.

Nootropic 🔬 Limited Western RCT Data

Semax

A synthetic derivative of ACTH researched for neuroprotective and cognitive-enhancing properties. Primarily studied in Russia for stroke recovery and BDNF upregulation. Limited Western-controlled trial data.
