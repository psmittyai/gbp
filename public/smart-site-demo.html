Connecting: Metformin timing + fasting window + post-workout AMPK pathway + mTOR suppression...

Great question — and these three things are actually fighting each other right now, which is why your workouts feel flat.

⚡ Here's what's happening (the part most coaches miss): Metformin activates AMPK — the body's "energy scarcity" signal — at the same time your fasting window is already running AMPK high. Then you're adding resistance training on top of that, which also spikes AMPK. The problem: AMPK directly suppresses mTOR, and mTOR is required to build muscle. You're hitting the gym but biologically blocking the adaptation signal.

The fix isn't stopping metformin or ending your fast — it's timing. Take your metformin at night (not morning), break your fast 90 minutes after your workout rather than before, and eat 40–50g of protein in that first meal. This lets your post-workout mTOR window open before metformin blunts it again.

I'd also add creatine monohydrate here — one of the few supplements that works via a parallel pathway to mTOR, so it bypasses the suppression entirely.

Can I build muscle while in a caloric deficit? ▾
Yes — but only under specific conditions. Body recomposition requires hitting 1g of protein per pound of lean body mass daily, resistance training that causes progressive overload, and adequate sleep (7+ hours minimum). In a moderate deficit of 300–400 kcal, most people gain muscle in months 1–3 before the hormonal environment shifts. After that, maintenance is more realistic. This window closes faster over 40.
Is intermittent fasting right for everyone? ▾
No. Women with hormonal sensitivity, people on certain medications (including metformin, thyroid meds), athletes with high training volume, and those with a history of disordered eating often do worse on strict fasting protocols. The research showing metabolic benefits at 16:8 applies primarily to sedentary, insulin-resistant men. For everyone else, time-restricted eating in a 12–14 hour window is safer and often more effective long-term.
What's the most underrated recovery tool Marcus recommends? ▾
Magnesium glycinate before bed, 300–400mg. It reduces cortisol's nighttime interference with deep sleep, supports muscle repair, and is depleted by both exercise and chronic stress — which describes almost every client Marcus sees. Cold exposure, HRV tracking, and NSDR protocols are all excellent, but none of them matter if your sleep architecture is broken. Fix the foundation first.
Do I need to track macros or just eat clean? ▾
"Eating clean" is too vague to produce consistent results. You need to know at minimum your protein intake and total calorie range. You don't have to track forever — 4–8 weeks of tracking builds intuition that most people carry for years. After that, visual portion estimation is usually sufficient. The exception: body recomposition, contest prep, and managing medical conditions all require ongoing tracking.
